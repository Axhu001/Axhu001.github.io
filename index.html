Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) & Natural Convection Analysis of an Electrical Busbar Enclosure

Method: Steady-state Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) modeling in ANSYS Fluent utilizing a buoyancy-driven Boussinesq model with PRESTO! pressure discretization and inflation boundary layers.

Results:

  • Volumetric source: 200,000 W/m³ per busbar (150 W total heat generation across 3 copper busbars)
  • Predicted peak busbar temperature: 516.4 K (243.4°C), with natural chimney draft velocities up to 0.408 m/s
  • Analytical validation using 1st Law energy balance and Churchill-Chu natural convection correlations (predicted 517 K, <0.2% error)

Insight: Thermal stratification creates progressive vertical wake preheating. The top busbar experiences the highest thermal penalty, while the buoyant plume accelerates into a high-speed boundary jet along the enclosure wall.

Design and CHT CFD Analysis of a Compact Pin-Fin Microchannel Cold Plate for High-Flux Electronics Cooling

Method: 3D steady-state laminar Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) simulation in ANSYS Fluent utilizing staggered micro-pin/rib turbulators for boundary layer disruption and thermal enhancement.

Results:

  • Thermal Equilibrium & Temperature Uniformity: The heat source reached steady-state thermal stabilization at Tmax ≈ 310.0 K (36.85°C) from an initial temperature overshoot of ~311.0 K.
  • Coolant Caloric Temperature Rise: Bulk coolant temperature increased from 300.0 K at the inlet to 304.3 K at the outlet.
  • Hydrodynamic Stabilization & Pressure Budget: Channel pressure drop converged to a stable ΔP ≈ 3.08 kPa after an initial transient numerical peak of ~10.6 kPa.
  • Local Flow Acceleration & Boundary Layer Tripping: Local fluid velocity increased from the uniform inlet velocity of 0.50 m/s to peak core velocities exceeding 1.25 m/s around the staggered pins.

Insight: The staggered pin-fin array acts as periodic turbulators that repeatedly disturb and re-initialize the hydrodynamic and thermal boundary layers. Wake mixing and throat acceleration reduce downstream thermal resistance without excessive hydraulic losses.
