Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) & Natural Convection Analysis of an Electrical Busbar Enclosure

Method: Steady-state Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) modeling in ANSYS Fluent utilizing a buoyancy-driven Boussinesq model with PRESTO! pressure discretization and inflation boundary layers.

Results:

  • Volumetric source: 200,000 W/m³ per busbar (150 W total heat generation across 3 copper busbars)
  • Predicted peak busbar temperature: 516.4 K (243.4°C) with natural chimney draft velocities up to 0.408 m/s
  • Analytical validation via 1st Law energy balance and empirical Churchill-Chu natural convection correlations (predicted 517 K, <0.2% error)

Insight: Thermal stratification creates progressive vertical wake preheating—the top busbar suffers the highest thermal penalty, while the buoyant plume accelerates into a high-speed boundary jet along the enclosure wall.

Design and CHT CFD Analysis of a Compact Pin-Fin Microchannel Cold Plate for High-Flux Electronics Cooling

Method: 3D steady-state laminar Conjugate Heat Transfer (CHT) simulation in ANSYS Fluent utilizing staggered micro-pin/rib turbulators for boundary layer disruption and thermal enhancement.

Results:

  • Thermal Equilibrium & Temperature Uniformity: The heat source reached steady-state thermal stabilization at Tmax ≈ 310.0 K (36.85°C) from an initial temperature overshoot of ~311.0 K, maintaining safe operational margins for high-heat-flux silicon dies.
  • Coolant Caloric Temperature Rise: Bulk coolant temperature elevated smoothly from 300.0 K (inlet) to 304.3 K (outlet) across the array, demonstrating efficient sensible heat extraction through the microchannel core.
  • Hydrodynamic Stabilization & Pressure Budget: Channel pressure drop converged monotonically to a stable ΔP ≈ 3.08 kPa after an initial transient numerical peak (~10.6 kPa), proving low pumping power requirements.
  • Local Flow Acceleration & Boundary Layer Tripping: Due to cross-sectional constriction (throat effect) around the staggered pins, local fluid velocity accelerated from the uniform inlet velocity of 0.50 m/s to peak core velocities exceeding 1.25 m/s, significantly boosting the local convection coefficient (h).

Insight: The staggered pin-fin array acts as periodic turbulators that repeatedly trip and re-initialize both hydrodynamic and thermal boundary layers. The induced wake vortex mixing and throat acceleration suppress downstream thermal resistance without imposing excessive hydraulic pumping penalties.
